Publish to the Marketplace

Share your apps, widgets, themes and components with everyone who builds with PadCode, on Pro: publish, follow them in Your Items, update or unpublish them, and report or block what you don't want to see.

Publishing to the Marketplace is part of PadCode Pro: on Free, Publish says so and offers the upgrade (see Plans and billing). Getting from the Marketplace is free for everyone, and everything in it is free. You can publish:

  • an app or a widget from your projects;
  • a theme you made in the Theme Builder or opened from a .padtheme file (not one that came with PadCode or from the Marketplace: those are someone else's);
  • a component from My Components.

#Community Guidelines

A person at PadCode looks at everything before it appears in the Marketplace, new items and updates alike, and again whenever someone reports it. The Publish sheet shows these guidelines, and you agree to them each time you send something.

Permanent ban, the first time, with no warning:

RuleWhat it means
Sexual content or nudityOf any kind, suggestive too: in screenshots, words, the item itself or anything it links to.
Anything that harms childrenSexualizing, exploiting or endangering minors. PadCode reports it to the authorities.
Hate or harassmentAttacking people for their race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, disability or age, or bullying anyone.
Violence, threats or self-harmThreats, gore, terrorism or extremism, or encouraging self-harm or suicide.
Scams and malwareTricking people into giving passwords, payment details or personal data, or harming their device.
Anything illegalSelling drugs or weapons, real-money gambling, pirated content, or anything else against the law.
Getting around the rulesHiding things from review, showing them after approval, or a new account after a ban.

Rejected (and again and again, a ban too):

RuleWhat it means
Someone else's workTheir apps, themes, artwork, logos, music or words, unless you have the right to use them.
Pretending to be someoneAnother creator, a real person, a brand, or PadCode.
MisleadingA name, tagline or screenshots that don't match what it does.
SpamCopies of your own items, empty or test items, and words stuffed in to be found.
Private informationAnyone's address, phone number, photos or other personal details.
Crude languageSwearing or slurs in names, taglines and descriptions.

What isn't approved comes back with a sentence saying why, so you can change it and send it again.

A ban is for good. You can't publish or rate anything again, everything you've published leaves the Marketplace (it shows as Taken down in Your Items, with the reason), and what's waiting for review is rejected.

#Publish something

  1. Open Marketplace from PadCode's sidebar and tap Publish at the top.
  2. Under What to publish, pick App, Widget, Theme or Component, then choose which one. Its name and icon come with it: to change them, rename it or change its icon in PadCode first.
  3. Write a Tagline: what it does, in a line (80 characters at most). An app or a component starts with its own description.
  4. Write About: what it does, how to use it, and anything people should know (2,000 characters at most).
  5. For an app, pick its Category.
  6. Look at the Screenshots. PadCode draws them for you, in light and dark: each screen of an app at an iPhone's size, a widget at Small, Medium and Large on a wallpaper, a component's pieces a page at a time, or PadCode's Builder in a theme's colors (a theme made for light or dark only gets that one). Tap ✕ on one to leave it out. Up to 6 go in; when there are more, the first 6 do.
  7. Read the Community Guidelines under the screenshots, and tick I've read the Community Guidelines. Nothing is sent until you do.
  8. Tap Send for Review.

Your Items opens, with your item In review. Once it's approved, it's in the Marketplace for everyone.

#Your Items

Your Items, at the top of the Marketplace, lists what you've published. Each shows where it stands, and once it's live, how many people got it and their stars.

BadgeWhat it means
In reviewIt's waiting for review. Nobody else sees it yet.
LiveIt's in the Marketplace.
Update in reviewYour update is waiting. People still get the live version.
Not approvedIt wasn't approved. The reason is under it.
Update not approvedYour update wasn't approved. The live version stays.
HiddenSeveral people reported it, so it's hidden until PadCode has looked at it.
Taken downPadCode took it out of the Marketplace. The reason is under it.
  • Update sends a new version: pick what it's made from (the same kind), change the words, agree to the Community Guidelines again, and tap Send Update. It's reviewed like a new item, and its version goes up by one (1.0, then 1.1) once it's approved.
  • … has the rest:
    • See in the Marketplace opens its page.
    • Withdraw Update takes back an update that's waiting, and leaves the live version as it is.
    • Unpublish takes it out of the Marketplace, with its ratings. People who already got it keep their copies. Something that was never live says Withdraw instead.

#Report or block

Tap … beside Get on an item's page:

  • Report sends PadCode what's wrong with it: Nudity or sexual content, Hate or harassment, Violence or threats, Something illegal, Someone else's work, Spam or misleading or Something else, and a few words more if you like. The creator isn't told who sent it. Something three people report is hidden until PadCode has looked at it.
  • Block hides everything by that person from you: their items and their page leave your Marketplace. It can't be undone yet.

A creator's page has … too, with Block. PadCode's own items can be reported but not blocked.

#Reviewing, for PadCode's reviewers

Reviewers see Review at the top of the Marketplace. It lists everything waiting: what's New, an Update to something live, and what's been Reported, with how many times.

  • Open opens it read-only, to tap through how it's made.
  • Approve puts it live. For something reported, Keep It leaves it live.
  • Reject asks why, in a sentence. The creator sees why, and can change it and send it again.
  • Take Down, for something reported, takes it out of the Marketplace, and its creator sees why.
  • In the Reject and Take Down sheets, Ban … for good is for the zero-tolerance rules: the button becomes Reject and Ban (or Take Down and Ban), and the creator can't publish or rate again, and everything they've published leaves the Marketplace.
