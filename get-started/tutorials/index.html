Tutorials

Guided tutorials inside PadCode: the tour, and one for each part of the app. Each one points at what to tap, and you do it for real.

PadCode can show you around itself. A tutorial dims the screen except for one thing, circles it, and says what it is and what to do, like "Tap New App". You tap the real thing, PadCode does what it always does, and the tutorial moves on to the next step.

#Open Tutorials

Tap Tutorials in the sidebar, just above Settings. In a narrow window it's in the More menu of the bar at the bottom.

  • New to PadCode? At the top, Take the tour shows you where everything is, in about two minutes.
  • Learn one thing at a time: a card for each tutorial, with what you'll learn and about how long it takes. Tap a card to start it.

Each card shows how far you've got: Done once you've finished it, or how many of its steps you've done, like "3 of 12 steps", with a bar. Once you've finished one, the line under Tutorials counts them, like "2 of 9 done". Starting a tutorial again starts it from its first step.

#Taking a tutorial

Each step has a card next to the part of PadCode it's about. The card shows the tutorial's name, the step's number (like 4 of 9) with a bar, what the part is, and what to do.

There are two kinds of step:

  • Your turn: the card says what to tap, and Your turn is where the button would be. Tap the circled part itself. PadCode does what it always does (a sheet opens, a view is added, Preview starts), and the tutorial goes on to the next step by itself. Only the circled part can be tapped; the dimmed rest waits. Once you've tapped it, the dimming steps aside, so a menu, a sheet or the keyboard it opens can be used too.
  • Next: the card explains something. Read it and tap Next (or Finish, on the last step).

A step where you type, like changing a Text's words in the Inspector, waits for you: Next appears once you've changed them, so tap it when you've finished typing. When the next step points somewhere else, the keyboard goes away by itself.

On every step:

  • Back goes back to the step before that still has something to do. Steps you've already done are passed over, so Back takes you to the last explanation.
  • Skip ends the tutorial. What you did so far stays done, and the card on Tutorials remembers how far you got.

#When what to tap isn't on screen

If the part a step is about isn't on screen (you went somewhere else, a panel is hidden, or it's scrolled out of sight), nothing is dimmed, so you can get to it, and the card sits in the middle of the screen:

  • Take me there goes to where it is: the right section, or the tutorial's app in the Builder.
  • Or the card says how to find it, like "The Library is the Builder's left panel: tap Show left panel, at the top left."

Once the part is back on screen, the step carries on.

#Sheets, the Builder and Preview

Tutorials work over everything PadCode shows: the New App sheet, the Builder, Preview, the Theme Builder, the icon designer and the Marketplace. Turning your iPad, or changing the size of PadCode's window in Split View or Stage Manager, moves the circle and the card with what they point at.

#Your work is safe

Tutorials never change your own apps. When a tutorial needs an app to work in, it makes a sample with "(Tutorial)" in its name:

TutorialIts sample
Your first appMy First App (Tutorial): New App's sheet is filled in with it, and you tap Create
A tour of the BuilderBuilder Tour (Tutorial), made when it starts
Data and actionsCounter (Tutorial), made when it starts
The AI AgentAgent Practice (Tutorial), made when it starts
WidgetsMy Widget (Tutorial): New Widget's sheet is filled in with it
Preview and InstallPractice App (Tutorial), made when it starts
The MarketplaceThe app you Get

A tour of PadCode and Make your own theme make nothing. Make your own theme doesn't save a theme: that's up to you.

#The end

When you finish a tutorial, PadCode celebrates with a check and a little sparkle, and says Tutorial complete.

If the tutorial made an app, the card names it and asks what to do with it:

  • Keep it keeps it in your projects, to go on playing with.
  • Delete it deletes it (and nothing else). If it was open in the Builder or in Preview, PadCode closes it and goes back to Tutorials. A widget you put in a slot leaves the slot too.

If you Skip a tutorial that made an app, the card says Tutorial skipped and asks the same. A tutorial that made nothing just ends, or shows Done after the last step.

#With a keyboard

Esc skips the tutorial, and on its last card it keeps what it made. It's Skip Tutorial in the menu bar's Help menu. Something that uses Esc itself takes it first: in Preview, Esc closes Preview (as Done editing does), and on the Builder's canvas it deselects.

#With VoiceOver

Each step's card is read out as it comes: its number, its title and what to do. Then:

  • Swipe to the card's buttons: Skip, Back and Next.
  • On a step that waits for you, the card's actions (swipe up or down) have Go to and the step's name: it moves VoiceOver to the part to use. Double-tap it, and the tutorial moves on.
  • The two-finger scrub (the escape gesture) skips the tutorial, and keeps what it made on its last card.

With Reduce Motion on, the circle doesn't pulse, the card doesn't glide from step to step, and the end has no sparkle.
